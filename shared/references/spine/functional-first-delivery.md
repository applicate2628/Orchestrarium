# Functional-first delivery — full reference

This is the detailed operational contract for the binding functional-first
delivery anchors in `shared/AGENTS.shared.md`. The spine is self-sufficient and
binding; this extract preserves the full acceptance, architecture, safety,
quality, correction-lineage, and commission detail without becoming a second
policy owner.

1. **Primary acceptance oracle.** Before implementation, freeze a Primary Acceptance Oracle (PAO) specification covering every admitted acceptance criterion and external contract and naming, for each, the exact target scenario and environment, invocation and steps, observable success and required failure semantics, safety preconditions, cleanup, evidence markers, owner, and method that identifies the evaluated source, configuration, and environment snapshot. Every PAO execution records the actual source, configuration, and environment snapshot. Mock or unit evidence cannot earn `Functional PASS`. A PAO specification or admitted functional scope revision revises the PAO ID and invalidates the affected gates; implementation alone never revises the PAO.

2. **Functional-first boundary; not code-first.** Functional correctness and a quality architecture foundation are co-primary delivery priorities; required architecture precedes implementation rather than becoming later hardening. Until `Functional PASS`, admit only diagnostics, tests, architecture/design, and changes causally linked to the PAO. When the admitted change creates or materially changes a shared or external contract, ownership boundary or stable seam, state or lifecycle owner, public API or schema, dependency direction, intended-reuse contract, or rollback guarantee, its proportionate architecture artifact and evidence-triggered design gate are part of functional scope and must precede implementation. At a variation point evidenced by an accepted requirement, a current second consumer, or verified external-contract evolution, choose the simplest one-owner contract and stable extension seam that satisfies the PAO and keeps the next accepted extension local; if no next extension is evidenced, do not optimize for one or implement hypothetical variants. Compare only designs necessary to resolve that gate, minimizing total lifecycle cost across implementation, verification, rollout or rollback, coupling, and blast radius. Urgency, quick delivery, or the hardening boundary never authorizes skipping the required foundation. Do not admit optional hardening, broad refactoring beyond those declared invariants, speculative edge cases, or unrelated review. A local correction that preserves those contracts and owners does not acquire an architecture ceremony merely from this clause.

3. **Bounded test-driven development.** Test-driven development (TDD) is allowed only for observed failure reproduction, declared acceptance conditions, verified-cause isolation, safe deterministic PAO execution, or repository-required checks; it never replaces or expands the PAO and never freezes unverified or workaround output into the contract.

4. **Evidence-connected safety stop.** Fail closed when evidence connects the current work to reachable confidentiality, integrity, authentication, authorization, trust-boundary, injection, untrusted-execution, data-loss, corruption, irreversible-action, or publication risk, and activate the existing risk owners. Only containment or remediation needed to resume may proceed; the stop grants no publication authority.

5. **Regression gate.** After `Functional PASS`, add the smallest deterministic regression guards, run repository-required and likeliest adjacent checks, and rerun the PAO. Any later relevant change invalidates the affected evidence.

6. **Hardening boundary.** Hardening is work beyond the declared functional contract, its proportionate evidence-triggered required architecture and extension seams, mandatory safety, and repository-required validation. Admit hardening separately only after `Functional PASS` and `Regression PASS`; otherwise keep it as nonblocking backlog. Implementing hypothetical variants or architecture/refactoring beyond the declared functional invariants and any evidence-backed accepted extension seam is hardening; a change to externally visible success or failure semantics is functional scope and restarts affected gates.

7. **One final QA package.** QA aggregates PAO results, regression and repository-required check results, snapshot identity, unchecked surfaces, and a triggered-reviewer table recording each trigger rule, its evidence, and whether its reviewer was invoked. A mandatory reviewer is triggered only by an existing `AGENTS.md` or repository rule or clause 4 evidence; ambiguity about whether such a trigger applies invokes that reviewer. Only those three cited bases constitute a real, gate-bearing veto under this policy; all other reviewer findings are non-veto improvements or nonblocking hardening and go to backlog. Neither the implementer nor $lead may waive or backlog a real, gate-bearing veto.

8. **Correction budget.** Two failed corrections based on the same hypothesis or a materially equivalent approach forbid a third use of that approach, not continued work on the task. Reset diagnosis across the root, owner, siblings, and affected surface, then use a new falsifiable approach, separately admit a prerequisite or redesign, or report a real external `BLOCKED`. A new correction budget requires a new hypothesis, a material difference from both failed approaches, a falsifying probe, and `$lead` acceptance of the affected surface.

8.1. **Stable lineage.** Keep gate, correction-budget, and backlog history under one stable lineage. A cited supersession may correct classification, seams, or acceptance without resetting the budget. Relabeling, splitting, or merging cannot erase or reset history or move an item between gate and backlog; derived work inherits that history. Each gate admission or exit under clauses 2, 4, 6, or 8 records one line of evidence against the frozen PAO ID. Resolve ambiguity into PAO scope or a mandatory veto.

9. **Publication safety unchanged.** Human publication approval and the leak scan remain mandatory and unchanged.

10. **Multi-model commission.** Require a genuinely multi-model commission only for a final candidate cross-cutting governance or design contract used by more than one workflow, owner, or module; ordinary local code fixes and inline corrections that preserve the accepted contract are excluded. Freeze the artifact before review and use at least two demonstrably different base-model families; differences in role, prompt, effort, alias, or variant do not count as model diversity. Record each runtime provider and model identity; unavailable required diversity returns a real external `BLOCKED`. Commission members independently evaluate whether the proposal's premises, boundary, consequences, lifecycle cost, and coupling are sound; user or author preference verifies intent, not technical correctness. Each member names accepted and rejected premises, offers a materially better alternative when one exists with its trade-off and `Would-flip-if`, and returns an evidence-based verdict. The synthesis records every verdict and resolves disagreement against the PAO, mandatory vetoes, and cited evidence rather than majority vote or deference; it is the sole candidate. Any synthesis change is re-reviewed on the exact delta by each affected commission member. Optional alternatives remain backlog and do not create another review round.

## Terms and Abbreviations

- `PAO`: Primary Acceptance Oracle, the frozen target-environment acceptance specification.
- `TDD`: test-driven development, bounded here to the listed evidence-serving uses.
