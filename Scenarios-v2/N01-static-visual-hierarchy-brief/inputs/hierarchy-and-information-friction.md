# Hierarchy And Information Friction

## High-cost failures

1. Curators miss unresolved reviewer asks because they look like ordinary notes.
2. Reviewers misread stale evidence as current because freshness is visually weaker than screenshots
   and bundle decorations.
3. Publish readiness appears as just another metadata badge instead of the top-level release
   verdict.
4. Operators cannot tell whether a problem is a blocking issue or a supporting detail without
   opening several drawers or tabs.

## Repeated complaints

- "I know the info is here somewhere, but I cannot tell what matters first."
- "Desktop says `Pending fixes`, web says `Open issues`, and neither one clearly wins the page."
- "The publish state looks too similar to ordinary labels."
- "Every surface makes me re-learn where the important facts live."

## Design pressure

The brief should solve hierarchy, grouping, and visual emphasis first. It should not spend most of
its energy on step-by-step flows, interaction choreography, or implementation details.
