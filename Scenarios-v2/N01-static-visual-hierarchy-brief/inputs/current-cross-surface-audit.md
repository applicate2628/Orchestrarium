# Current Cross-Surface Audit

## Desktop routing workspace

- The left pane mixes bundle identity, pack metadata, and archived notes with equal weight.
- The main center area begins with screenshots and fixture counts, while validation blockers often
  appear several scrolls lower in expandable cards.
- The right inspector shows owner, lane, evidence freshness, comments, and publish prerequisites in
  one undifferentiated metadata stack.
- The visual treatment for a blocking validation failure matches the treatment for stable facts like
  pack ID or scenario count.

## Web release console

- The queue emphasizes preview thumbnails and bundle names before review blockers or unanswered
  reviewer questions.
- The detail page opens on an overview tab that leads with history and screenshots rather than the
  current release decision.
- Publish readiness, evidence freshness, and reviewer asks are split across separate tabs with no
  shared summary ladder.
- The web console uses `Needs clarification`, `Open issues`, and `Review blockers` for states that
  the desktop surface calls `Questions`, `Validation blockers`, and `Pending fixes`.

## Cross-surface effect

Operators mentally rebuild the hierarchy every time they switch surfaces. The bundle is the same,
but the information structure is not.
